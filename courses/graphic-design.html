Quick Answer

This is a 12-week course for people who have never done design before. You learn the basics of layout, colour and type, then work in Photoshop, Illustrator and Canva. You build a logo and brand kit, a month of social media posts, and a set of print files, and put them on Behance.

Week-by-Week Syllabus — Graphic Design

6 modules · 3 months (12 weeks · 96 hours of instruction)

Module 1Design Basics: Layout, Colour and Type

Weeks 1-2
  • What a graphic designer does, with examples you see daily: shop boards, hoardings, Instagram posts, menu cards
  • Layout: alignment, spacing, grids, and how to decide what the eye should see first
  • Colour: the colour wheel, matching colours, contrast, and what colours make people feel
  • Type: serif and sans-serif fonts, pairing two fonts, sizes that are easy to read on a phone
  • Moodboards and reference pictures: how to collect ideas without copying anyone
  • File types in plain words: JPG, PNG, PDF and SVG, and when to use which
  • Practice task: fix a badly made sweet shop poster and explain every change
CanvaGoogle FontsCoolorsPinterest

Outcome: Look at any poster or post, say what is wrong with its layout, colour and fonts, and redo it so it is clear and easy to read.

Module 2Photoshop for Photos and Posters

Weeks 3-4
  • The Photoshop screen, layers and why you should never work on a single flat layer
  • Selection tools and masks: cutting a product out of its background cleanly
  • Basic photo fixes: brightness, colour cast, removing dust spots and small marks
  • Text, shapes and simple effects that look neat and not crowded
  • Pixels and resolution: why a photo looks blurry when you stretch it
  • Making a product poster for a clothes shop or a cloud kitchen offer
  • Saving files for WhatsApp, Instagram and print, with the right size and quality
Adobe PhotoshopPexels (free photos)Google Fonts

Outcome: Edit a photo, cut out a product, and finish a clean poster in Photoshop with organised layers.

Module 3Illustrator, Logos and Brand Kits

Weeks 5-6
  • Vector and pixel pictures: why a logo must be a vector file
  • Shapes, the pen tool and Pathfinder, taught step by step with lots of practice
  • Taking a logo idea from a pencil sketch to a clean digital logo
  • Logo versions: full colour, black and white, and a small icon for the profile picture
  • Choosing brand colours and fonts, and writing them down in a one-page brand kit
  • Designing a visiting card and a simple letterhead
  • Common logo mistakes: too many colours, thin lines, copied icons
Adobe IllustratorCanvaCoolors

Outcome: Design a simple logo from a sketch, prepare all its file versions, and hand over a one-page brand kit to a small business.

Module 4Social Media Design in Canva

Weeks 7-8
  • Canva in detail: grids, frames, layers, shortcuts and a brand kit set up once and used every time
  • Right sizes: Instagram post, story, reel cover, YouTube thumbnail, WhatsApp status
  • Changing a template so it looks like your client and not like everyone else's
  • Carousel posts: a first slide that makes people swipe and a last slide that asks for action
  • Festival and offer posts for local business, such as Diwali sale and Navratri offers
  • Writing short text for posts: clear headline, one offer, one way to contact
  • Planning one month of posts for a business so the page looks the same every day
CanvaAdobe PhotoshopMeta Business Suite

Outcome: Design a full month of matching social media posts for a local business in Canva, in the correct sizes.

Module 5Print Basics and AI Image Tools

Weeks 9-10
  • Print terms explained simply: bleed, margin, trim marks and why text near the edge gets cut
  • RGB for screens and CMYK for print: why colours look dull when you convert
  • Paper sizes and uses: A4, A5, visiting card, pamphlet, flex banner
  • Designing a poster and a pamphlet, then checking the file before it goes to the press
  • Talking to a local printer: what file and settings they need
  • Using AI image tools for ideas, backgrounds and small fixes
  • Using AI responsibly: copyright, copying an artist's style, fake product photos, and telling your client when AI was used
Adobe IllustratorAdobe PhotoshopAdobe FireflyChatGPT

Outcome: Prepare a poster and a pamphlet that a printer can use without any changes, and use AI images only where they are honest and allowed.

Module 6Freelance Portfolio, Behance and Career Prep

Weeks 11-12
  • Choosing your best three projects and writing a short, honest case study for each
  • Setting up a Behance profile and uploading projects with good cover images
  • Preparing a short PDF portfolio you can send on WhatsApp or email
  • Resume and LinkedIn profile for a designer, with your portfolio link
  • How freelance work usually goes: brief, first draft, feedback rounds, final files
  • Mock client pitch: showing your work and answering the client's questions
  • Mock interview and a final review with your mentor
BehanceCanvaLinkedIn

Outcome: Publish a Behance portfolio with three finished projects and present your work to a client or an interviewer with confidence.

Frequently Asked Questions — Graphic Design

Is graphic design worth learning in 2026?
Every business needs posts, posters, logos and menus, so there is steady work for people who can do it well. AI tools make rough images quickly, but someone still has to decide what is clear, correct and right for the brand. That is the part you learn here. Results depend on your practice and your portfolio, and we cannot promise a job.
What is the fee for the Graphic Design course?
Fees are shared by our advisor on a call or on WhatsApp. EMI-style options are available on enquiry. We do not put a fee on the website, so you get the current details for your batch directly.
Do I need any prior knowledge or drawing skill?
No. The course starts with the basics of layout, colour and type. If you can use a computer and a phone, you can start. Drawing skill helps a little with logos, but it is not needed.
Is the course offline in Nagpur or online?
You can choose. We run it offline at our Ramdaspeth centre in Nagpur, online live on Zoom, or as a hybrid. Batches are small, and 1-on-1 mentor sessions are part of the course in every mode.
How is this different from learning from YouTube videos?
YouTube is good for single tricks. It does not give you an order to learn in, a teacher to check your work, or someone to say your poster looks crowded. Here you follow a plan, finish real projects, get feedback in class and in mentor sessions, and end with a portfolio.
I am from commerce or arts. Can I join?
Yes. Many design students come from commerce, arts and other non-IT streams. The course does not need coding or maths. Your own taste and the habit of looking carefully matter more.
Does the course cover UI/UX design, video or InDesign?
No. This course is about graphic design for logos, social media and print. UI/UX design is part of our web designing course, and video is in our video editing course. We would rather teach fewer things properly.
Will I get a certificate?
Yes. You get a Course Completion Certificate — Skilleze Technologies. The certificate shows that you finished the course. What clients and employers will really look at is your portfolio.
Can I use AI image tools for client work?
We teach you to use them carefully. AI can help with ideas, backgrounds and small fixes. You should read each tool's rules on commercial use, avoid copying a living artist's style, never show a fake product photo as the real product, and tell the client when AI was used.
